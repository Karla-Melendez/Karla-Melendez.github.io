Not long ago, I thought up the type of users my Aquascape Vision app should target. As a tech writer, I’m used to writing 
documentation aimed at different levels of users. All software systems will have regular end-users—the type of people who use the 
software in their day-to-day work—and system administrators. Further, depending on the type of software system, for example, those 
used in call centers, the end-users could be divided into different personas, such as agents and their supervisors.

So the notion of designing an app for different user personas wasn’t something extraordinary in my mind. What was a little more 
difficult was coming up with the exact type of personas. There will be no system administrators or employee supervisors. Everyone 
using my app will be regular aquarium hobbyists, some with more experience than others. 

The challenge then, for me, was in figuring out the needs, design objectives, and what success would look like for each of the 
different types of personas who would benefit from my app, and craft a profile for them. In terms of profile, I thought 
information such as the size of tank they’d typically own would be useful. 

I came up with three types:

- **Novice:** new to the aquarist hobby, may have kept fish before, is only now getting into the aquascaping art.
- **Intermediate:** has planted aquariums before, knows what’s involved in the upkeep of aquatic plants, would like to learn more about how to make the aquarium look polished.
- **Advanced:** at the competition level.

And here's a quick introduction to my three targeted personas.

**Bo Rivera** is brand new to planted aquariums–usually working with a 10–20 gallon tank, overwhelmed by conflicting advice 
online, and just hoping their first attempt doesn't fall apart.

**Niko Hernández** has a few aquascapes under their belt, is comfortable in the 20–75 gallon range, and is past 
"will this work"—now chasing better composition and fewer rescapes down the line. (This is roughly where I sit myself.)

**Ari Nakamura** operates at a near-competition level, works in tanks from 60 to 180 gallons, and isn't looking for guidance so 
much as tools that speed up planning and iteration.

What ties the three together, more than tank size or experience, is what each one is afraid of: Bo fears failure. Niko fears 
wasted effort. Ari fears wasted time. Designing well for all three means designing against three different fears, not just three 
different skill levels.
